# Borrowed from other OptChat builds, 14 October 2026

Taelin's gist has spawned some forty implementations. These changes come
from the ones that measured something or fixed a gap opt-chat shared. Each
names its source.

## Recall: raw messages that share the user's words

A summary line keeps a message's gist and drops much of its detail. Before
answering, each turn now gets up to 8 messages from the whole log, given
raw. They are ranked by the words they share with the user's message, and
rare words weigh the most. This is the default; `-recall 0` turns it off.

- **Words:** a word is three or more letters or digits, lowercased, and
  matches anywhere in a message, as `search` does.
- **Weight:** each word weighs the square of its BM25 inverse document
  frequency, ln((N − df + ½) / (df + ½)), with no negative weights. A word
  in half the messages or more weighs nothing.
- **Score:** a message scores the sum of the weights of the words it
  holds, scaled by its length as BM25 scales a word found once (k1 1.2,
  b 0.75). Ties go to the newer message. Hits scoring under a quarter of
  the best are dropped.
- **Why these changes:** omp-optchat uses the plain weight with no length
  scaling. On a copy of a real chat of 28,500 messages, that ranked long
  pastes full of middling words ("please", "answer", "sentences") above
  the two short messages holding the question's one rare word
  ("Superwhisper"). With the squared weight and the length scaling, those
  two came first. Opus then answered a question about them correctly,
  dates and model names included, with no tool call. 92% of that turn's
  input was read from the cache.
- **Left out:** the chat's own `zoom`, `search` and `date` calls and their
  results, and messages the view already shows whole.
- **Shown:** each hit is cut to 1,200 bytes around its rarest word.
- **Where it goes:** the turn's input, after the view's cached part, ahead
  of the user's message. It is not logged.
- **What it searches with:** the user's words, including a voice message's
  transcript and the text of an inlined file.
- **Cost:** about 0.2 s over 27,000 messages, run while the call starts,
  and no model call.

The source is [omp-optchat
0.9.0](https://github.com/ericjuta/omp-optchat/releases/tag/v0.9.0). Its
`chat_recall` tool scores view lines with a judge model, and from 0.9.0 it
also takes the 8 best-matching raw messages as candidates. On its recall
eval this answered 65 of 96 questions against 21. Raw messages won 72
times and were right 62 of them; view lines won 24 times and were right 3.
Its summaries kept the fact for only 11 of 48 gold messages.

opt-chat skips the judge and hands the raw messages to the turn. That is
cheaper, but the gain has not been measured here. Opt may get messages
that are beside the point, and the header says so.

## Smaller fixes, after pi-optchat

- **A tool result's compaction sees its call**
  ([#171](https://github.com/jonaslsaa/pi-optchat/pull/171)): the call it
  answers goes in as `<call>`, up to 4,000 bytes. pi-optchat measured
  recall going from 3.88 to 4.22 with it.
  - **How opt-chat finds the call:** by its Ref. Imports from Claude Code
    and Codex now keep their tool ids as Refs.
  - **Messages logged before Refs were kept:** they are paired by place. A
    run of calls made at once is answered by the next run of results, in
    the same order, and the pairing is used only when both runs are the
    same length.
- **A view batch cut short survives a restart**
  ([#180](https://github.com/jonaslsaa/pi-optchat/pull/180)): `view.json`
  records it.
- **Claude Code imports** ([#162](https://github.com/jonaslsaa/pi-optchat/pull/162)):
  - Only queued prompts from the user count: task and agent notifications
    are left out.
  - Array-form prompts are read too.
  - A queued message handed back by an Esc comes in once:
    - When the next prompt sends it again, the queued copy is dropped.
    - When it doesn't, the queued copy is kept, since the model saw it.
      This is a change from pi-optchat, which drops it either way.

## The prompt cache

opt-chat already put what changes per turn (Claude Code's date and cwd, the
input) after the view's cached part. Others log their cache hits per
request ([umiddey/omp-optchat](https://github.com/umiddey/omp-optchat),
[elephant-memory](https://github.com/Shtukbase/elephant-memory)), and they
report 97 to 99% of input read from the cache. The page's bar now shows the
last turn's share, so a broken cache shows up there.

## Not taken

- **Three lengths per compaction**
  ([cube #81](https://github.com/cubeyard/cube/pull/81)): the compactor
  writes its line at three lengths and keeps the longest that fits. There
  it cut calls per line from 2.67 to 1.03.
  - **How it was measured here:** 24 lines (16 messages, 8 merges) from a
    copy of a real chat, built both ways on Sonnet at
    `medium`.
  - **Results:** opt-chat's ruler already took 1.38 tries a line, against
    1.00 with three lengths. Output tokens rose from 7.0k to 10.7k, and the
    lines were no longer (414 against 421 bytes on average).
  - **Why not:** a second try here is another request in the same call,
    which reads the call from the cache, so the extra output costs more
    than the tries it saves.
- **Merging down to 85% of the budget**
  ([shitty-optchat](https://github.com/gebeer/shitty-optchat), D13 in its
  SPEC): its fold had no hysteresis and merged at almost every message.
  opt-chat already merges down to half (128 KB to 64 KB), which batches
  merges further apart. Going to 85% would rewrite the cached view about
  three times as often.
- **pi-optchat's mid-turn refresh**
  ([#170](https://github.com/jonaslsaa/pi-optchat/pull/170)): it moves a
  long turn's steps into memory and goes on from the view. That is
  compaction of the main chat, which opt-chat never does.
